Research explainer / Condition A

When the answer is in the world,
does the model find it?

We give a model a complete, synthetic world of facts and relationships. Its task is simple to state: decide whether a conclusion follows. We vary the graph’s depth and breadth, then measure where reliable conclusions break down.

Unreachable does not mean absent. The target is in both worlds. In the False case it lies in the other disconnected component. The reference algorithm finds no path from the entity; the model must reach that conclusion from the supplied text.


Why use synthetic worlds, and what do depth and breadth mean?
  • Opaque, freshly generated symbols reduce direct factual-recall shortcuts. They do not rule out familiarity with the task type.
  • Depth counts implication hops along a spine. The entity’s starting fact adds one graph edge.
  • Breadth is the number of children per internal predicate, not the maximum width of a graph layer.
  • The world family contains two spines with distractor leaves. This is not a test of every possible graph topology.
  • The displayed pair is an actual recorded example, selected to illustrate a false positive, not a representative sample. Aggregate results below include every planned case.

03 / HOW TO INTERPRET IT
A measured reliability limit, not a universal capacity score.
What the evidence supports
Under the tested prompt, representation, and model-specific settings, all four models have measured settings above 90% successful correct rejection and settings below it.
Both unsupported conclusions and missed supported conclusions occur. Providing all required information is not sufficient to guarantee a correct answer.
What it does not establish
These are not production-safety limits, a ranking of intrinsic model intelligence, or a smooth boundary across untested graphs. Models used different complexity neighborhoods and provider settings.
Prompt effects remain unresolved: the True-only format example, asymmetric proof instructions, opaque labels, and rule ordering may contribute. They require separate controlled tests.


Statistical method, study provenance, and reproducibility
This is a retrospective joint analysis of completed confirmation cohorts, not a prospectively registered four-model study. All 20 correct-rejection settings are included. Two-sided exact Clopper–Pearson intervals use Bonferroni correction with tail α = 0.05 / 40. For C known successes and U unknowns, the lower limit uses C successes and the upper limit uses C + U successes out of 2,000.
The family covers successful correct rejection only. Explicit false-positive rates, reachable accuracy, proof validity, and model superiority are not additional jointly corrected claims. Shared worlds across models are paired. The analysis assumes independent sampled world units within each model-cell.
Luna used native OpenAI Batch with medium reasoning effort; Gemma, Hermes, and DeepSeek used DeepInfra Batch with their recorded settings. Each call had a 24,000-token output ceiling. Condition C and GLM are not included. The illustrated graph is a rendering of a recorded Condition A input, not an extra tool available to the model.
The page is an offline snapshot of completed results. No inference runs or external network requests are triggered by its controls.
